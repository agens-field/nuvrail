# CompanySOUL.md
## Key Takeaways from Paul Graham's "How to Start a Startup"
_Source: https://www.paulgraham.com/start.html_

---

## The Three Things That Matter

1. **Start with good people**
2. **Make something customers actually want**
3. **Spend as little money as possible**

Every startup that fails breaks at least one of these. That's it. No magic.

---

## People

- **Hire "animals"** — people who take their work past professional and into obsessive. If you can't describe someone as an animal at what they do, don't hire them.
- **Smart + gets things done + bearable to work with.** All three. Non-negotiable.
- **Founders: 2–4.** One person can't bear the moral weight. Too many creates factions. You need unanimity, not voting.
- **Tech startups need technical founders.** Business people can't evaluate what's hard or easy in software.
- **The most important skill: understanding users.** If you don't have it, either learn it or find a co-founder who does. This is the rock that sinks more startups than anything else.

---

## The Idea

- **Ideas are starting points, not assets.** The market price of a startup idea, without the people to execute it, is essentially zero. VCs won't even sign an NDA for one.
- **Good people fix bad ideas. Good ideas can't save bad people.**
- **Look for where things suck most.** That's where the opportunity is. Corporate IT, enterprise software — these are slums most good hackers have never visited. Enormous money, terrible products.
- **Niche markets beat consumer brands.** The odds are better. Low end eats high end — always.

---

## What Customers Want

- **Build what people actually want.** This sounds obvious. Almost no one does it.
- **Prototype fast. Put it in front of users. Iterate.** There is no other way to know if you've got it right.
- **Hail Mary strategies fail.** Elaborate planning + big engineering teams + one-year buildout = two million dollars to make something nobody wants.
- **Ease of use is underrated.** A 10% improvement doesn't drive 10% more sales — it can double them. Most hackers vastly underestimate how hard normal people find software.
- **Watch your users.** Not just listen — watch. Trade shows, support calls, direct observation.

---

## Money

- **Seed comes from angels.** Informal, fast, doesn't need a polished business plan. A half-page agreement and a demo is often enough.
- **VCs are slow and dilutive.** Approach early, before you're out of runway. Take the most money with the fewest strings.
- **"Newscasters" are dangerous.** Polished, confident, know nothing. VCs sometimes want to install one as CEO. Resist this.
- **Valuation at seed stage is a bet, not a calculation.** Don't be paralyzed by it.
- **IP history matters.** Check everyone's prior agreements before you consummate the company. This can kill you.

---

## Not Spending It

- **Burn slow.** The slower you spend, the more time you have to learn.
- **Don't hire to fill an org chart.** Only hire someone to do something you want done that you literally cannot do yourself.
- **Small headcount is strength, not weakness.** Twenty sharp people beating a hundred mediocre ones is impressive. Don't confuse headcount with progress.
- **Grad student mentality, not law firm.** Cheap office, cheap chairs, cheap everything. Money is not revenue; it's runway.
- **Get big slow.** First users who find you on their own are your best users. They'll tell you exactly how to make the product better — and pay you for the privilege.

---

## Nuvrail Implications

These principles cut directly to what we're doing:

- **We are building for people who have real pain.** Enterprise buyers who've handed AI agents raw email access and lost sleep over it. That's where things suck. That's our market.
- **Don't over-engineer Phase 0.** Prototype, get it in front of real users, watch them, iterate.
- **Team quality over team size.** KC and Stella are the right people. We stay lean.
- **The trust narrative IS the product.** Understanding what our users actually fear is not a marketing problem — it's the core design problem.
- **Spend slow.** We are not revenue yet. Every dollar is runway. Protect it.
