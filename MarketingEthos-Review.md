## Suggestions

### 1. The contractor analogy vs. the git analogy — pick a primary

The doc uses two analogies: the contractor with a master key, and the pull request model. Both are good. But the contractor is intuitive to a business buyer; the pull request is intuitive to a developer. We should decide which audience we're leading with and make that analogy the primary one, with the other as a supporting frame.

My recommendation: **lead with the PR analogy.** It's more precise, more original, and it scales better across both audiences than the contractor metaphor does. The contractor is familiar but it's been used. "The pull request model for AI email access" is genuinely new language.

### 2. Competitive section — add one more differentiator

We name Nylas (raw access) and Microsoft Copilot/Google (closed ecosystem). Worth adding a third category: **workflow automation tools** (Zapier, Make, n8n). A lot of teams are currently trying to solve this problem with Zap + Gmail + a Slack approval bot. That's the DIY incumbent we're actually competing with in the short term, and naming it helps the buyer feel seen.

### 3. The "bonded professional with a logbook" line

This appears in the Voice section but doesn't show up in the core narrative. If it's a real analogy we're using, it should either be developed fully (in docs, website, press kit) or cut from this document. Half-introduced analogies create confusion. My vote: cut it here and revisit it as a potential long-form content angle.

### 4. Social tone — slightly expand

"Sparse. We are not a social-first company." I agree with the instinct but this gives no one anything to work with when they need to post something. Suggest adding one sentence: what *does* a Nuvrail post look like when it means something? (A product milestone? An insight about AI agent risk? A customer story?) Even a single example would make this actionable.

### 5. One addition to "What We Don't Do"

Consider adding: **We do not call our customers "users."** Enterprise buyers are decision-makers and their trust is the product. Language like "teams," "customers," and "buyers" keeps the relationship accurate.
